The True Cost of In-House vs Outsourced Customer Support

Universal Solutions Team · Updated September 2026

Comparing in-house and outsourced customer support usually starts with salary numbers — but salary is only part of the real cost. A fair comparison needs to account for everything that goes into standing up and running a support function.

Costs that come with building support in-house

  • Recruiting and hiring — job postings, interview time, and the opportunity cost of an open seat
  • Training — onboarding time before a new hire is fully productive
  • Tooling — help desk software, telephony, and other support infrastructure
  • Management overhead — team leads or managers dedicated to overseeing the function
  • Turnover — support roles often have higher attrition, meaning the hiring and training cycle repeats often
  • Coverage gaps — extending hours (nights, weekends) usually means hiring more staff or paying overtime

What outsourced support typically includes

An outsourcing partner usually bundles staffing, training, tooling knowledge, and management into a single engagement. Instead of separately budgeting for each of the items above, a company pays for the output — tickets resolved, calls handled, coverage hours delivered — and the partner absorbs the underlying operational overhead.

Where outsourcing tends to cost less

Outsourcing often reduces costs most clearly in three areas: reduced hiring and training cycles (since the partner already has trained staff), lower management overhead (since oversight is largely handled by the partner), and more flexible scaling (since capacity can expand or contract without a formal hiring process each time).

Where in-house support can still make sense

For highly specialized or judgment-heavy support — where deep, company-specific product knowledge is essential to every interaction — an in-house team may be worth the investment. The right choice often depends on how standardized the support workflows are, and how much of the work benefits from external training versus internal-only knowledge.

A more useful comparison than salary alone

Rather than comparing a support agent's outsourced hourly rate to an in-house salary, it's more useful to compare total cost per resolved ticket or per hour of coverage — including hiring, tooling, management, and turnover on the in-house side. That comparison usually tells a more complete story.
